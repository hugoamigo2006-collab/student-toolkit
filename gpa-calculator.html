How is GPA calculated?

GPA is calculated using a weighted average of your grade points and the number of credits for each course.

GPA Formula

GPA = Total Grade Points ÷ Total Credits

Each course contributes according to the number of credits it carries. A course worth more credits has a greater effect on your overall GPA.

GPA Calculation Example

Imagine you have three courses:

Mathematics: A (4.0) × 3 credits

History: B (3.0) × 3 credits

English: A- (3.7) × 2 credits

Total grade points = 12 + 9 + 7.4 = 28.4

Total credits = 8

GPA = 28.4 ÷ 8 = 3.55

What is GPA?

GPA stands for Grade Point Average. It is a numerical representation of a student's academic performance.

Many schools and universities use a 4.0 scale, although grading systems and GPA calculations can vary by institution.

Always check your school's official grading policy if you need an exact GPA conversion for academic purposes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good GPA?

On a 4.0 scale, a GPA around 3.0 or higher is often considered strong, while a GPA of 3.5 or above is commonly considered very good. However, expectations vary by school, program and country.

What is a 4.0 GPA?

A 4.0 GPA generally represents the highest point on a standard 4.0 grading scale and commonly corresponds to an A average.

Does every university use the same GPA scale?

No. Universities and schools can use different grading systems, scales and conversion methods. This calculator uses a common 4.0 scale.

Does a course with more credits affect my GPA more?

Yes. GPA is normally calculated using a weighted average, so courses with more credits have a larger effect on your overall GPA.
